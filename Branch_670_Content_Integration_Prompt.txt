TASK: COMPREHENSIVE CONTENT REVIEW, EXTRACTION AND INTEGRATION — BRANCH
670 PRESENTATION

1.  OBJECTIVE

I am providing: 1. A link to a folder containing numerous presentations
related to the organization and its activities. 2. The original Branch
670 presentation, which serves as the master presentation and the
structural basis for the final deliverable.

Your task is to review all relevant presentations in the folder,
identify the most accurate, informative, relevant, and well-written
content, and integrate it into the corresponding slides of the original
Branch 670 presentation.

The goal is to transform the original presentation into a complete,
accurate, professional, and informative presentation that explains the
organization’s mission, responsibilities, capabilities, value chain, and
outputs.

Do not simply copy text between presentations. Understand the
information, assess its relevance, and integrate it intelligently.

2.  ANALYZE THE ORIGINAL PRESENTATION SLIDE BY SLIDE

Before reviewing the source presentations, read the entire original
Branch 670 presentation.

Examine every slide individually and identify: - The purpose of the
slide. - The information it is intended to communicate. - What
information is already present. - What information is missing or
insufficiently explained. - What supporting information is needed to
complete the slide. - The appropriate level of detail for the intended
audience.

Treat every slide as a specific content requirement. Do not rely only on
slide titles. Analyze the actual content, headings, layout, and intended
message.

Create an internal content map connecting each original slide to the
information that needs to be found in the source presentations.

3.  REVIEW THE PRESENTATIONS IN THE PROVIDED FOLDER

Thoroughly examine the presentations in the linked folder. Do not rely
only on filenames, slide titles, or introductory slides.

Read the actual slide content, including: - Headings and subheadings. -
Text and descriptions. - Diagrams and organizational charts. - Processes
and workflows. - Value-chain illustrations. - Responsibilities and areas
of ownership. - Operational vectors. - Outputs and deliverables. -
Tables, charts, and visual explanations.

Use OCR or visual inspection when text is embedded in images or
diagrams.

Review all relevant presentations, not just the first few files. Work
systematically through the folder and maintain a record of the
information extracted from each source.

4.  PRIORITY CONTENT TO EXTRACT

A. ORGANIZATIONAL MISSION AND PURPOSE

Find the clearest and most accurate descriptions of: - The
organization’s overall mission. - Why the organization exists. - The
need it addresses. - Its primary responsibilities. - Its contribution to
the broader organizational mission.

Identify concise, strong mission statements suitable for an executive
presentation.

B. MISSION AND PURPOSE OF EACH DEPARTMENT

For every department, identify: - Its core mission and reason for
existence. - Its primary responsibility. - Its professional domain. -
Its unique contribution to Branch 670. - Its main areas of ownership.

A purpose statement should explain why the department exists, not merely
list its activities.

C. OWNERSHIP OF THE VALUE CHAIN

This is one of the highest-priority areas.

Identify which parts of the value chain are owned or managed by each
department. Look for: - Stages of the value chain. - Responsibilities at
each stage. - Ownership of development processes. - Data and information
flows, where explicitly documented. - Infrastructure and systems
responsibilities. - Product ownership. - Processing and
information-production capabilities. - The relationship between
development activities and final outputs.

Clearly distinguish between: - Full ownership. - Partial
responsibility. - Supporting capabilities. - Shared responsibilities,
where explicitly documented.

Do not assume that a department owns an entire process simply because it
participates in it.

D. OPERATIONAL VECTORS

Identify the organization’s main operational vectors and areas of
activity: - What each department is responsible for. - What it develops
and operates. - Which capabilities it provides. - Which professional and
technological domains it covers. - How its responsibilities are defined.

Organize these vectors clearly and consistently.

E. OUTPUTS AND DELIVERABLES

Identify the actual outputs produced by the organization and its
departments. Look for: - Information products. - Technological
capabilities. - Systems and platforms. - Algorithmic outputs. - Data and
processing capabilities. - Product deliverables. - Other documented
outputs.

Explain what each department produces and what value those outputs
provide.

Do not confuse activities, capabilities, systems, and final outputs.
Treat them as distinct categories.

5.  ORGANIZATIONAL BASELINE — BRANCH 670

Use the following information as the organizational baseline when
analyzing the source material.

Branch 670 operates in the field of visual intelligence.

Its activities include two independent product domains:

VIDEO / VISUAL DOMAIN - Software development for the video domain. -
Data pipeline development. - End-user systems. - Dedicated product
management. - Dedicated video algorithmics teams within the AI
department.

IMAGERY INTERPRETATION DOMAIN - Software development for imagery
interpretation. - Processing pipeline development. - Storage
capabilities. - End-user systems. - Dedicated product management. -
Dedicated imagery interpretation algorithmics teams within the AI
department.

AI DEPARTMENT - Separate algorithmics teams for video and imagery
interpretation. - A team responsible for algorithmic pipelines. - A
Fullstack unit responsible for making algorithmic outputs accessible.

Important organizational distinctions: - Video and imagery
interpretation are separate product domains. - Their systems,
development processes, and outputs are independent. - Their algorithmics
teams are separate and professionally independent. - AI must not be
described as creating a dependency or operational connection between the
two domains.

Use this information to understand the organization, but rely on the
source presentations for additional details and specific descriptions.

6.  CONTENT SELECTION AND INTEGRATION

Do not copy every slide or every paragraph from the source
presentations.

Select information based on: 1. Relevance to the original slide’s
purpose. 2. Accuracy and specificity. 3. Clarity and quality of writing.
4. Professional value for an executive audience. 5. Contribution to
understanding the organization.

When multiple presentations describe the same subject: - Compare the
descriptions. - Identify the most informative and well-articulated
version. - Preserve important details that appear in only one source. -
Avoid unnecessary repetition. - Do not silently combine conflicting
descriptions.

Prefer specific, meaningful descriptions over generic corporate
language.

Use the strongest source material as the basis for final wording, making
only the edits necessary for clarity, consistency, and presentation
quality.

Do not invent information, responsibilities, outputs, processes, or
organizational relationships.

If information is missing, contradictory, or unclear, flag it for review
rather than making assumptions.

7.  MAP SOURCE CONTENT TO THE ORIGINAL SLIDES

The original Branch 670 presentation remains the master presentation.

For every slide: - Identify what information is required. - Locate
relevant information in the source presentations. - Select the strongest
supporting material. - Adapt the wording to fit the slide. - Complete
missing information only where reliable source material exists. -
Preserve the slide’s original purpose and message.

If relevant information is found in a source presentation but does not
fit any existing slide, identify it as a potential addition.

Do not add slides automatically. Evaluate whether the information is
genuinely necessary.

The final presentation should tell one coherent story, rather than look
like a collection of copied material.

8.  WRITING AND PRESENTATION STANDARDS

The audience consists of senior executives and business partners.

The content must be: - Professional. - Precise. - Informative. -
Strategic. - Clear to people who are not familiar with internal
terminology.

Use concise executive-level language without losing important
professional detail.

Avoid: - Long paragraphs. - Repetitive explanations. - Generic
slogans. - Vague descriptions. - Unexplained internal terminology.

Where appropriate, use: - Clear mission statements. - Responsibility
descriptions. - Value-chain breakdowns. - Operational vectors. - Outputs
and deliverables. - Process diagrams. - Structured comparisons.

9.  PRESERVE THE ORIGINAL DESIGN

The original Branch 670 presentation is the design and structural
reference.

Do not redesign the presentation as part of this task.

Preserve: - Existing slide structure. - Established Apple-inspired
visual language. - Bright background. - Subtle Glassmorphism style. -
Sharp rectangular geometry. - Typography and color palette. - Overall
visual identity.

Adapt the amount of text to the existing layouts without compromising
readability.

If a slide requires more information than its current layout can
accommodate, suggest a content or layout adjustment rather than
shrinking the text excessively.

10. SOURCE TRACEABILITY AND ACCURACY

Maintain a reference for every significant piece of information added to
the original presentation.

Record: - Source presentation filename. - Source slide number. -
Information extracted. - Destination slide in the original presentation.

If two sources contradict each other, flag the discrepancy and identify
both sources.

Do not present inferred information as established fact.

11. FINAL QUALITY CONTROL

CONTENT - Every original slide has been reviewed. - Relevant source
information has been incorporated. - Departmental missions are clearly
explained. - Value-chain ownership is accurately described. -
Operational vectors are presented. - Outputs and deliverables are
clearly identified. - There is no unnecessary duplication. - All claims
are supported by source material.

CONSISTENCY - Terminology is consistent throughout. - Each department is
described at an appropriate level of detail. - The presentation tells
one coherent organizational story. - The information is understandable
to external business partners.

PRESENTATION - The original design language is preserved. - Slides
remain readable. - No slide is overcrowded. - Diagrams and visual
explanations accurately represent the source material.

12. FINAL DELIVERABLES

Provide:

1.  THE COMPLETED BRANCH 670 PRESENTATION Integrate relevant information
    from the source presentations into the original slides.

2.  A SLIDE-BY-SLIDE CONTENT INTEGRATION SUMMARY Explain what was added
    or improved on each slide and identify the source presentation and
    slide number.

3.  A LIST OF INFORMATION GAPS AND CONTRADICTIONS Identify subjects that
    could not be completed because source material was insufficient,
    unclear, or conflicting.

FINAL INSTRUCTION

Approach this task as an organizational analyst, an executive
presentation writer, and a content editor.

The objective is not to create more slides or add more text. It is to
make every slide in the original presentation more informative,
accurate, meaningful, and professionally articulated by extracting the
best available content from the source presentations.

Be thorough. Review the entire source folder, understand the material,
and only then integrate the findings into the original Branch 670
presentation.

Do not guess or fill gaps with general knowledge. Base the integrated
content on what the source presentations actually support, and
explicitly flag anything that remains unknown or contradictory.
